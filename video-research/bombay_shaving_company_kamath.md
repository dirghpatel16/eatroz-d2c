## Question asked
What GTM, distribution, branding, and profitability lessons does Shantanu Deshpande give about building Bombay Shaving Company into a profitable ₹500 Cr brand? Extract concrete numbers, tactics, and do's/don'ts for an Indian D2C brand.

## Response

### 1. Financial & Scale Metrics
₹500 Cr net revenue with a lean 170-180 employee team. Profitable on both EBITDA and PAT, cash-flow positive. Current-year EBITDA exceeds total 2021 top-line revenue. 99% of revenue still India; international next, leveraging diaspora brand pull.

### 2. Product & Pricing Playbook
Category pivots: niche premium shaving → men's grooming → post-COVID body-hair-removal (trimmers, women's hair removal). Extreme price architecture: ₹1 single blades (100-pack ₹100, targeting barbers/schools) up to ₹7,000 premium trimmers. Premium-looking branding at surprisingly affordable price points to drive conversion.

### 3. Customer Acquisition & Behavioral Insight
Men: "hard to engage, easy to please" — brand-loyal once habituated, hard to switch. Women: "easy to engage, hard to please" — will try new brands but demand high performance to stay. The trimmer hook: older men (25-30+, 10+ years on Gillette) almost never switch — so target teenagers (14-17) at their *first* grooming-tool purchase (₹500-1,000 trimmer today vs. a ₹25 razor at 18 a generation ago), building a 4-year brand runway before their first razor purchase. Gen-Z tactics: heavy Instagram, Red-Bull-style college activations, student ambassadors, pop-up "barbershops for young people."

### 4. Category Dominance
~7,000 trimmers/day sold nationally at ~₹1,000 average price; ~15% market share of the Indian trimmer market.

### 5. Offline/General Trade advice
DO: test small first — one city/neighborhood around a high-performing dark store before scaling; require strong brand pull + assortment sharpness + competitive pricing before offline placement.
DON'T: rush into General Trade unprepared — burns capital fast without proven offline sell-through velocity.

## Relevance to Eatroz
The "catch them at first purchase" logic doesn't map directly (Eatroz's audience is 18-35 adults, not a first-purchase-at-14 category), but the broader lesson — extreme price-point discipline (a clear entry SKU, not a muddled range) and testing offline in one contained geography before national GT rollout — reinforces this project's existing single-SKU-first, prove-before-scale GTM sequencing (Part J7/J9).
