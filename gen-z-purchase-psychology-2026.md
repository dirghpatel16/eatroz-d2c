## Why this cohort buys supplement gummies at all

- **Anxiety is the actual driver, not "wellness" in the abstract.** Over 70% of Gen Z adults report daily anxiety/stress. Stress & mood supplements are the fastest-growing segment in the category, and gummies specifically win because they read as a *ritual/treat*, not a chore — "taking a berry-flavored gummy with L-theanine or ashwagandha feels more like a treat than medicine." This is a direct, sourced validation of magnesium-for-sleep's whole premise as a *ritual*, not just a dose delivery mechanism. [Source](https://meetglimpse.com/trends/supplement-vitamin-trends)
- **Format preference is real and measurable**: 38% of Gen Z and 30% of Millennials favor gummies over traditional pill/capsule formats. [Source](https://www.menshealth.com/nutrition/a70943188/gummie-vitamins-supplements)
- **Gen Z is a small but disproportionately valuable supplement-buying cohort**: 4% of buyers, 17% more spend than Gen X. 48% purchase vitamins at all (vs. 31% who report zero supplement purchases in six months) — a real, not universal, addressable market. [Source](https://intermountainnutrition.com/gen-zs-role-in-supplement-demand)

## Price sensitivity — the actual shape of it, not the stereotype

- Gen Z is **less** broadly price-conscious than older generations (36% self-identify as price-conscious vs. 45% of Gen X) — but "the ones who do spend big, go big." [Source](https://www.gwi.com/blog/generation-z-characteristics)
- The mechanism is **"functional affluence"**: willingness to pay premium specifically for products that combine a lifestyle/status signal with a real functional/clinical benefit — not premium for its own sake. [Source](https://www.dbbnwa.com/gen-z-prioritizes-premium-wellness-and-beauty-spending-in-2026)
- A 2026 retail survey found **61% of Gen Z respondents are specifically reallocating budget toward fitness/health purchases** even while tightening spend elsewhere — described as a "reset mentality," proactive rather than reactive wellness spend. [Source](https://www.dbbnwa.com/gen-z-prioritizes-premium-wellness-and-beauty-spending-in-2026)
- **India-specific proxy data** (fashion, not supplements directly, but same cohort/market): 47.5% of Indian Gen Z respondents say they'll pay a premium for a brand whose *values* align with theirs, specifically citing sustainability — willingness to pay is real but conditional on genuine value-alignment, not brand polish alone. [Source](https://www.firstresort.in/blogs/research/gen-z-millennial-indian-fashion-spending-2026)
- Direct India wellness signal (from an investor panel, not a formal study): "wellness" and "pharmacy and wellness" were named as the categories with the most growth in the last two years, with D2C specifically called out as having billion-dollar-brand potential. Thin as a source (a panel soundbite, not a report) but directionally consistent with everything else in this repo's research. [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BUlvivTqSTo)

## What actually influences the purchase decision — and what doesn't as much as assumed

- 85% of Gen Z say social media affects purchase decisions generally — but for supplements *specifically*, only **12% say influencers directly sway their supplement purchase** (a much lower rate than Gen Z's general influencer-susceptibility stat would suggest). [Source](https://intermountainnutrition.com/gen-zs-role-in-supplement-demand)
- Ranked higher than influencers, even for Gen Z broadly: family/friends (56%), product reviews (54%), third-party review sites/forums (47%). [Source](https://www.retaildive.com/news/generation-z-social-media-influence-shopping-behavior-purchases-tiktok-instagram/652576)
- **Unboxing videos and product reviews specifically build trust through perceived authenticity/detail**, not hype — a real, cited academic finding (Ortiz et al. 2023), not just a marketing claim. [Source](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/influence-of-tiktok-trends-on-the-buying-intentions-of-gen-zs)
- **A live cautionary tale worth internalizing directly**: fibre supplements are having a Gen Z moment, and brands are already "leaning into inflating claims and anti-fat messaging" to capture it — early signs of the same claims-inflation/scepticism cycle this project has already flagged for magnesium (Man Matters' reformulation-around-a-claim history). Gen Z notices and punishes this fast. [Source](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/beauty/gen-z-fibre-supplements-loam-hum-nutrition)

**Net read for Eatroz's GTM (not a new strategy, a confirmation of the existing one):** low direct influencer-purchase-sway + high trust in unboxing/reviews/peer opinion argues for the nano/micro-creator, real-usage-integration GTM already recommended in `strategy.md` Part J7/J11 over paid-influencer volume — this research independently reinforces that call rather than changing it.

## 2026 Packaging Design Trends — direct relevance to DESIGN.md

Several trends found are genuine validation of decisions already locked; one is a real, worth-flagging tension.

### Validates the existing direction
- **"Apothecary" is explicitly named as a live 2026 trend**, not a dated register: "botanical visuals + apothecary design cues... sage green, dusty rose, amber, cream... classic serif fonts (Garamond, Baskerville, Caslon)... symmetrical layouts echoing old pharmacy filing systems." This is close to a direct description of Eatroz's own "Botanical Clinical" territory — genuinely reassuring that this isn't reading as dated, it's reading as current. [Source](https://confetti.design/blog/packaging-design-trends)
- **"Transparent and honest labelling"** is named as its own numbered 2026 trend, independent of the supplement-specific research already in this repo — a third independent confirmation (after The Whole Truth and Ritual/Seed) that transparency-as-design-principle is a real, current, broad packaging trend, not just a supplement-category tactic. [Source](https://www.designerpeople.com/blog/packaging-design-trends-2026)
- **Sustainability has shifted from differentiator to baseline expectation** — worth noting as an open item for the `eatroz-packaging-spec` skill to consider (recyclable pouch material, not just print/seal spec) even though it wasn't explicitly in the original brief. [Source](https://premiumlabelandpackaging.com/2026-packaging-trends-wellness)
- **Smart packaging (QR/NFC)** is named as a 2026 trend specifically for dietary supplements — a natural, low-cost extension of the existing "commission and publish lab test results" plan: a QR code on-pack linking directly to the published Certificate of Analysis would combine two things this project already wants to do. Worth logging as a concrete implementation idea for `eatroz-packaging-spec`. [Source](https://summitrxusa.com/blogs/blogs/top-3-trends-in-dietary-supplement-packaging-for-2026)

### Real tension worth flagging honestly, not adopting
- **"Candid Camera Roll" aesthetic** — grainy film textures, light leaks, hand-drawn doodles, deliberately imperfect typography — is named as a genuine 2026 Gen Z/Millennial trend specifically because it "feels personal, intimate, and relatable... values transparency over perfection." This is in real tension with Eatroz's precise, controlled, near-zero-border-radius design language. **Not a recommendation to adopt it** — Eatroz's transparency claim is structural/data-driven (the ring dial), not aesthetic-imperfection-driven, and mixing the two registers would blur the brand rather than strengthen it. But worth knowing this exists as a live alternative resolution of the same "we value transparency" goal, in case a social-content-specific sub-style (Instagram Stories, not the core brand system) ever wants a looser register than the core pack/site identity. [Source](https://imaginity.com/design-process/leading-packaging-trends)
- **Personalization/limited runs** (on-demand printing, name-on-label, "Spotify Wrapped"-style personalized packaging) — a real trend, but almost certainly premature for Eatroz's Phase 0/1 (per the pitch deck's GTM sequencing) — flagging as a Phase 2+ idea, not a current gap.
