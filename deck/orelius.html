01 · Consumer problem

Daily nutrition has an adherence problem

Many consumers want a health routine but do not want pills, powders, shakers, or a cupboard full of single-benefit products. Convenience can create a habit only when the product also earns trust.

Orelius targets the gap between clinical-format supplements and candy-led gummies.
  • Powders create preparation and taste friction
  • Single-benefit gummies can feel interchangeable
  • Large ingredient claims leave consumers unable to judge real potency
  • Repeat purchase decides whether the category becomes a business

Inference from current product positioning and founder evidence. See Slides 3, 6, 15 and the source ledger.

10 · Formula development gates

The product earns its claims through finished-product evidence

Gate 1

Eligibility

Review every ingredient, level, additive, warning, and intended claim under the applicable Indian framework.

Owner: regulatory lead
Gate 2

Stability

Measure active retention through shelf life under relevant heat and humidity conditions.

Owner: formulator + lab
Gate 3

Quality

Assay actives and test microbiology, heavy metals, and relevant contaminants on finished batches.

Owner: quality lead
Gate 4

Claims

Approve packaging and creator guidance before commercial use; retain the evidence trail.

Owner: regulatory + marketing

Verified regulatory boundary: FSSAI Health Supplements and 2022 operationalised framework. Development gates are proposed.

11 · Credibility as a product feature

The pack should answer the sceptical customer’s first question: what is actually inside?

  • Amount-declared ingredients rather than a marketing-only blend
  • Lot-specific finished-product report link
  • Plain-language guidance on use, warnings, and limits
  • Claims that match the evidence and the permitted category

Little Joys and Kapiva show that Indian brands already use quality-report communication. Orelius can adapt the principle for adults only after it has its own finished-product evidence.

“60°C heat-stable” stays a development objective until a specific finished-product study supports the exact claim.

Benchmark: Little Joys; Kapiva. Orelius system: proposed.

12 · Initial customer thesis

Target a repeatable occasion before targeting a demographic

18–35

Initial audience hypothesis: urban Indian adults with health intent and low tolerance for a complicated routine.

  • Morning or desk-side daily pack
  • Busy weeks when meal routines break down
  • Trial through trusted creators and direct evidence
  • Reorder because the habit feels useful and easy, not because a discount expires

Audience is a proposed research hypothesis. Validate through qualitative interviews, landing-page conversion, first-order surveys, and actual second-order cohorts.

20 · Investment thesis

Orelius can test an Indian daily-nutrition habit with more discipline than a gummy clone

The product thesis: a transparent, lower-sugar, pectin daily pack that makes routine nutrition easier to follow.

The business thesis: a controlled D2C validation phase that earns expansion through finished-product quality, contribution, and repeat purchase.

The investor decision: fund proof of a real habit and operating system. Do not fund a promise that a public ingredient list alone creates scale.

Next action: appoint formulation/regulatory owners, close the diligence gaps, and design the first retention experiment.
