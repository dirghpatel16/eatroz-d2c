# Functional Wellness Gummies: Packaging & Brand Architecture

## Executive conclusion

The category has split into two successful, but incomplete, codes. US leaders make adherence feel pleasurable: Grüns turns supplementation into a portable snack ritual; OLLY turns a benefit into a bright emotional cue; Lemme turns it into beauty culture; AG1 turns it into a performance identity. India has proven demand for convenient formats and bright benefit-led packs, but its winning trust cues remain explicit active ingredients, dosage, FSSAI visibility, clean-label markers, and recognizable functional form factors.

The opportunity is **evidence-made-desirable**: a brand that has the appetite appeal and thumbnail speed of a modern gummy brand, but the disciplined information architecture, dosing clarity, and climate robustness of a serious nutrition company. The recommended launch bet is **Option A as the masterbrand**, with **Option C’s daily-dose sachet system** as a premium subscription/quick-commerce extension. Option B is the strongest acquisition engine; Option D is a differentiated India-origin platform, but should not be the first masterbrand unless the formulation has genuinely distinctive botanical authority.

This report distinguishes observed facts from strategic interpretation. Product availability, prices, claims, and packs change frequently; the audit reflects sources accessed 12 September 2026.

### 1.3 Brand portrayal: the important distinction

The strongest brands do not merely list nutrients. They make a choice about what a daily health action *means*.

- **Grüns:** “A treat that behaves like nutrition.” The 28-pack system, upbeat language, flavors, and collaborations turn repetition into novelty. Its science and testing content is the credibility counterweight, not the visual starting point.
- **OLLY:** “Feeling good is permitted to be easy and delightful.” It organizes the assortment by emotional end-state, then supports that with science and NSF/quality language.
- **Ritual:** “You should interrogate your vitamin.” Transparent sourcing, clinically framed testing, and restrained visual codes make skepticism part of the brand proposition.
- **Lemme:** “Wellness belongs on the vanity and in culture.” The appeal is editorial, feminine and collectible; this works only when efficacy proof is visible enough to prevent it reading as pure merch.
- **AG1 / Create / Supply6:** “Your daily system should reduce cognitive load.” The hero is not one ingredient; it is behavioral consistency, dosing, and a better routine.
- **Wellbeing Nutrition:** “Technology can make nutrition feel frictionless.” Fast-dissolve oral strips, precision dose, no-water use, and patented/clinically validated ingredients are all part of the same technology story.
- **Plix / What’s Up Wellness / Power Gummies:** “You can solve a visible concern in a pleasant format.” The approach creates highly legible acquisition packs, but can slide into a candy aesthetic where every benefit looks interchangeable.
- **Kapiva / OZiva / HK Vitals:** “Choose your authority.” Kapiva borrows from heritage and testing; OZiva from clean plant-based wellness; HK Vitals from broad, pragmatic nutrition.

### 1.4 What not to copy

1. **Candy without evidence.** A bright jar, cartoonish fruit, and too many soft claims can earn trial but suppress perceived potency and price tolerance. Power Gummies is useful as a caution—not because a bright pack is inherently weak, but because the category now has many bright jars claiming similar beauty/weight/sleep outcomes. The brand needs a visible reason to believe: quantified active, form, standardization, third-party testing, or a credible protocol.
2. **Clinical without a hero.** White packs often vanish as thumbnails and on dense shelves. Minimalism must be designed as an optical signature—one ownable color, silhouette, or device—not merely absence of design.
3. **Ingredient wallpaper.** Botanical collages, fruit splashes, icons, certification stamps, and benefit bubbles compete rather than clarify. Limit the front to one primary promise, one proof unit, and one format cue.
4. **Imported premium codes without Indian clarity.** In India, an elegant abstract pack that omits dosage, ingredient quantity, vegetarian status, FSSAI/legal panel, or how-to-take can read as cosmetic rather than credible nutrition.

## 2. US versus India: the contextual design gap

| Decision | US D2C / Target / Whole Foods / Sephora context | India D2C / Blinkit / Zepto / Nykaa / Amazon India context | Implication for the new brand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium cue | Edited restraint, material quality, transparency, cult-cultural relevance, certification. | Proof density, visible actives/dosage, clean/veg status, FSSAI legitimacy, value per day, plus premium finish. | Design a two-layer pack: a calm premium upper field plus a rapid proof strip. |
| 2-second recognition | Shelf blocking and silhouette can work because physical browsing is slower. | A 1:1 thumbnail competes with hundreds of products and a discount/promise-heavy grid. | The brand name, core benefit, format, and one numeric proof must be readable at 120–160 px. |
| Habit device | Subscription, ritual kit, reusable canister, travel packs. | Replenishment, trial packs, cash-efficient entry price, office/gym portability, heat resilience. | Begin with 30-day jar or 15-day starter; add sealed daily doses for subscriptions and travel. |
| Trust language | “Traceable,” “clinically tested,” “third-party certified,” low sugar. | “Clinically studied ingredient,” exact dose, vegetarian/vegan, no artificial colour, FSSAI, lab report/QR, manufacturing quality. | Use a QR-led certificate of analysis and simple evidence cards; never make the front feel like a prescription label. |
| Sensory desire | Texture, flavor, lifestyle image, cultural collab. | Flavor familiarity, everyday utility, affordable premium, local relevance. | Let flavor be sensorial but secondary to the functional benefit on first launch. |

### Blinkit/Zepto thumbnail rules

- Build and approve the FOP at **140 px wide** before approving it at actual size. If the benefit cannot be read in one glance, it has failed.
- Use no more than **three information zones**: brand, outcome, proof/format.
- Place product-type words in the top two-thirds: “GUT HEALTH GUMMIES,” “DAILY MULTI,” or “MAGNESIUM NIGHT CHEWS,” never only on a side panel.
- Favor one strong hue family per SKU, plus a constant masterbrand signature. Avoid pale labels on clear jars unless the cap/body silhouette does the work.
- Do not lead with vague territory labels such as “wellness,” “balance,” or “glow.” Lead with a named job-to-be-done, then allow the brand world to carry the emotion.

This is not merely an aesthetic preference: recent quick-commerce operating guidance emphasizes durable, tamper-resistant, compact packs that work on app thumbnails, while marketplace onboarding guidance specifically flags high contrast and thumbnail testing as a category requirement. Treat the exact platform image rules as a launch checklist to validate with the account manager rather than a permanent universal specification. ### Target / specialty retail shelf rules

- The pack must make a **three-foot block**: common bottle/pouch geometry, large product name, and high color distinction by benefit.
- The product needs a physical reason to pick up: soft-touch label, a satisfying cap/closure, a partially visible gummy, a tactile carton, or a dose-count cue.
- Use shelf-ready secondary packaging only where it adds an unboxing/retention benefit. In India, avoid a disposable premium carton around a basic jar unless it supports heat, tamper evidence, or giftability.

## 3. Non-negotiable packaging platform

### Recommended architecture: one brand, two pack modes

**Mode 1 — 30-day heroic jar (launch core).** An opaque or UV-protective recycled PET jar with induction seal, tamper-evident shrink band, high-barrier liner, and a wide but controlled opening. This is the best launch format for gummies: familiar, credible, fast to photograph, and resilient to humidity when correctly specified. Use a 150–200 cc squat cylinder or soft-square silhouette; avoid a generic supplement bottle shoulder.

**Mode 2 — 7-day / 14-day daily-dose stick or pillow sachet (subscription and travel).** Metallized high-barrier laminate with a strong seal specification suitable for Indian heat/humidity. Use it for high-value protocols, samples and travel—not as the only initial SKU, unless the consumer gets a tangible freshness or dosing advantage. A 30-sachet system has more material and filling complexity; price it as a premium routine, not as a cost-saving pack.

**Material and tactile specification.** Matte BOPP or soft-touch varnish label, selectively glossed product/benefit word, deboss-like tactile varnish only if it survives mass printing, and a single metallic accent reserved for the masterbrand. Do not use foil everywhere: it quickly becomes “beauty nutraceutical” generic. Use PCR where compatibility tests permit; stability, seal integrity, and moisture barrier take priority for gummies.

**Climate and fulfillment.** Gummies are heat/humidity sensitive. OLLY itself advises cool, dry storage and notes softening/clumping under heat/humidity; it also describes packaging compatibility, barrier, seal, and stability testing. For India, specify accelerated stability and distribution testing under the actual shipper/carton configuration, not just the jar. Consider desiccant only after validating sensory and regulatory compatibility. [OLLY FAQ](https://www.olly.com/pages/faq) · [OLLY science](https://www.olly.com/pages/science)

**Compliance discipline.** Treat the FOP as a brand surface; reserve a dedicated legal panel for FSSAI-required declarations, net quantity, ingredient/nutrition, recommended use, warnings, batch/expiry and marketer/manufacturer information. Validate copy and claims with Indian regulatory counsel before artwork release. Do not let a Runway concept image imply unsubstantiated medical outcomes.

## 4. Four brand prototype directions

The names below are working territories, not clearance-checked trademark recommendations.

## Option A — Modern Apothecary / Clinical Chic

### Strategic bet

**Working territory: FORM / Proof, made daily.** This is a sophisticated evidence-first wellness brand for urban Indian consumers who want the confidence of a clinical product without hospital or pharmacy coldness. It can credibly price above bright mass-premium jars and extend into strips, capsules, powders, and skincare-adjacent ingestibles.

**Who it recruits:** 25–42, ingredient-literate, digitally research-active, buys skincare/health through Nykaa, Amazon, quick-commerce and D2C; distrusts influencer miracle claims but wants an enjoyable daily ritual.

**Brand voice:** Assured, exact, quietly warm. Use short declarative phrases: “Dose matters.” “Designed for daily use.” “Evidence, made easy.” Never use “detox,” “miracle,” “fix,” or overpromised transformation language.

### Visual system

| Element | Blueprint |
| --- | --- |
| Master palette | Chalk `#F4F1EA`, deep ink `#132421`, mineral sage `#60766D`, signal citron `#D8E54C`. |
| SKU coding | One mineral accent per need: gut `#7BA6A2`, sleep `#5F5A99`, beauty `#C8848E`, energy `#D9894A`, multi `#D8E54C`. Keep 70% of every pack masterbrand-neutral. |
| Typography | Primary: a high-legibility neo-grotesk (e.g., Söhne/Neue Haas/Inter alternative). Secondary: restrained humanist serif for one emotional line only. Numerals must be tabular and large. |
| Mark | A calm wordmark with an optional calibration line/device—never a medical cross or leaf. Use a measured “F” monogram / molecular dot as a non-verbal recognition asset. |
| Image world | Macro translucent gummy, frosted glass, soft natural morning light, lab-grade paper texture; real people only in calm editorial portraits. |

### Pack architecture

**Primary form:** opaque milky-white 180 cc soft-square PCR PET jar, satin black or dark forest child-resistant cap if required, induction seal, low-gloss wrap label. It should feel like a premium dermocosmetic object rather than a candy jar.

**Front panel hierarchy:**

1. `FORM` (large, top left; brand owned across all SKUs)
2. `GUT DAILY` (large, 2–3 words max)
3. `10B CFU + [named prebiotic]` (one precise proof line; formulation dependent)
4. `30 DAILY GUMMIES · [flavor]` (small but legible)
5. Small vertical citron strip: `LAB-VERIFIED / QR` (use only when substantiated)

**Back panel:** a generous “Why this works” block, exact active amounts, dose/how-to, taste note, ingredient source/proof, QR to batch test, and legal declarations. Use a modular grid—no icon confetti.

**Tactility:** soft matte base; clear gloss only on `GUT DAILY` or a slim calibrated band. Embossing belongs on an optional secondary carton, not a launch jar label.

### Portfolio and commercial behavior

- Begin with three legible need-states: **Daily Multi**, **Gut Daily**, **Night Magnesium**. Do not launch hair, skin, weight, hormones, immunity, and stress simultaneously.
- Use the accent color as a navigation stripe, never an all-over jar color. The masterbrand should look coherent in an Amazon carousel.
- 7-day starter pack: a compact recyclable paper sleeve holding 7 individually sealed daily doses. It earns a premium because it demonstrates a protocol, not because it is “more packaging.”
- Thumbnail test: `FORM` plus `GUT DAILY` must remain readable against white marketplace backgrounds. Use the citron calibration band as the recognizable object in a dense grid.

### Failure mode and guardrail

Failure: it could look too international/sterile and be mistaken for a cosmetic serum. Guardrail: show exact dosage and “GUMMIES” above the fold; use a visible translucent gummy on one panel and a warm flavor descriptor.

**Hero-film prompt:** “9:16 premium wellness campaign, a milky-white soft-square supplement jar with a satin dark forest cap, minimal chalk label, deep ink typography, a single citron calibration stripe, translucent pale-green gummies, morning sun moving across limestone and frosted glass, quiet editorial composition, clinical confidence without hospital imagery, macro product cinematography, slow 50mm push-in, natural shadows, no readable text, no logos.”

## Option B — Functional Confectionery / Dopamine Wellness

### Strategic bet

**Working territory: GOOD MOOD GOODS / Feel it, take it.** This is a high-acquisition, high-shareability brand that treats the gummy as a small daily delight, not a compromise. It can win on Zepto/Blinkit thumbnails, creator content, flavors, and limited-edition drops. Its non-negotiable is a serious proof system that prevents it becoming just another bright candy jar.

**Who it recruits:** 18–32, digitally native, routine-resistant, buys beauty and snacks impulsively, wants concerns stated in plain language and will share an object that feels like culture.

**Brand voice:** Smart, witty, never juvenile. “Your 3 p.m. reset.” “Tiny habit. Big energy.” “Feel-good, with receipts.” Avoid childish puns and body-shaming language.

### Visual system

| Element | Blueprint |
| --- | --- |
| Master palette | Black cherry `#27142A` or warm cream `#FFF3DD` as the anchor; flavor/benefit colors are intentionally loud. |
| SKU coding | Sleep: electric lavender; gut: acid lime; energy: mango orange; beauty: hot guava pink; multi: cobalt. Use two-color combinations, not rainbow gradients. |
| Typography | Oversized soft geometric sans with a sharp condensed technical companion for dose/proof. The contrast says “fun outside, formula inside.” |
| Mark | A chunky wordmark with a gummy-shaped punctuation mark or custom ligature. No cartoon mascot at launch. |
| Image world | Flash photography, oversized fruit, tactile sugar sparkle, glossy tabletop, friends in real-world micro-rituals. |

### Pack architecture

**Primary form:** a short, wide, opaque color-tinted rPET jar with a matte opaque lid; label field in cream or dark cherry to prevent pure color overload. A pull-tab induction seal creates satisfying first-open content.

**Front panel hierarchy:**

1. `GOOD MOOD GOODS`
2. `SLEEPY TIME` / `GUT CHECK` / `ENERGY CHEWS` (very large)
3. One bold proof: `300 MG MAGNESIUM` / `10B CFU` / formulation-approved equivalent
4. `30 CHEWS · MANGO [flavor]`
5. A small corner “VEG / NO ARTIFICIAL COLOURS” only when true.

**Proof mechanic:** create a consistent **Receipts panel** on the back: active amount, source/form, quality standard, serving, QR to testing. It is visually in the same design language as a festival ticket or receipt—not a clinical interruption.

### Portfolio and commercial behavior

- Launch with two emotional, low-ambiguity problems: **Sleep** and **Gut**; then `Daily Multi`. Beauty and weight should wait until the brand has earned trust.
- Flavor names should be sensory but not obscure: “Mango After Dark,” “Guava Glow,” “Citrus Reset.” The clinical product name remains clear on FOP.
- Own limited drops through color and flavor only; never change the core hierarchy. A collaboration sleeve over the jar is preferable to a new permanent visual system.
- For quick commerce, use a simple all-color packshot and repeat the outcome in the listing title. For retail, arrange colors in a deliberate spectrum with dark-cherry caps as a shelf unifier.

### Failure mode and guardrail

Failure: read as candy, invite sugar/efficacy skepticism, or blur into the existing bright-jar field. Guardrail: quantify one hero active on every FOP, keep a 20–30% neutral field, and publish a visible proof/quality system.

### Runway visual direction

**Hero-film prompt:** “9:16 high-fashion functional confectionery campaign, vivid guava pink and acid lime supplement gummy jars with matte dark-cherry caps, oversized translucent gummies bouncing on a glossy cream table, ripe mango and guava arranged sculpturally, energetic direct flash, joyful young adult hands, modern editorial styling, playful but premium, no readable text, no logos, precise product geometry.”

## Option C — High-Performance Daily Ritual / Biohacker Clean

### Strategic bet

**Working territory: DAY//ONE / Make the habit automatic.** This is the performance-system brand: measured, portable, data-aware, and subscription-native. It has the greatest advantage for repeat behavior and a high AOV, especially if the formulation is a defensible multi-benefit daily gummy or a companion powder/strip protocol.

**Who it recruits:** 24–40, gym/fitness/wearable users, founders and knowledge workers, values time, tracks habits, wants clean design without macho sports clichés.

**Brand voice:** Precise, energizing, low-drama. “One daily signal.” “Built for repeat.” “Your baseline, covered.” Do not claim biohacking superiority or use jargon consumers cannot decode.

### Visual system

| Element | Blueprint |
| --- | --- |
| Master palette | Carbon `#111516`, bone `#E8E8E0`, signal green `#B8FF3D`, data blue `#86C7FF`. |
| SKU coding | Keep the carbon field fixed; use one thin edge/color code by protocol: baseline green, focus blue, recovery orange, night violet. |
| Typography | Monospaced/technical secondary used sparingly with a modern grotesk primary. Large numbers are a distinctive asset. |
| Mark | `DAY//ONE` or equivalent typographic system with a double-slash timing device. The logo must remain human, not crypto/AI startup. |
| Image world | Athletic recovery, laptop-to-gym transitions, gym bag, water, early light, modular grids, movement data as subtle overlays. |

### Pack architecture

**Primary form:** individual high-barrier daily stick/pillow sachets in a flat 30-day dispenser carton; optional refill pouch and reusable anodized/aluminum-look countertop caddy. If gummies cannot be packed together without quality compromises, use blister-like sealed wells or two-gummy mini sachets validated for moisture and temperature.

**FOP hierarchy:**

1. `DAY//ONE`
2. `BASELINE` (or explicit need state)
3. `1 DAILY PACK · 30 DAYS`
4. `21 NUTRIENTS + 6G FIBER` (only as formulation permits)
5. bottom microline: `GUMMY DAILY NUTRITION`

**Unboxing:** inner sachets show day count (`01/30` etc.), a minimal timing cue, and a colored protocol edge. Outer carton includes an “Open → take → repeat” strip. This is behavior design, not decoration.

### Portfolio and commercial behavior

- Best core is a **single hero system**, not an instant family of products. Add `NIGHT` only after the baseline repeat pattern is established.
- D2C subscription gets reusable caddy + refill; quick-commerce gets a seven-day trial sleeve and a 30-day carton. Retail gets a compact shelf-ready carton with side-facing benefit data.
- The hero metric must be honest and stable: active grams, number of nutrients, or daily packs—not a shifting marketing superlative.
- Build content around “day in the life,” airport/commute/gym bag, and habit streaks; avoid medicalized before-and-after claims.

### Failure mode and guardrail

Failure: overengineered sachets raise cost, feel too male/fitness-centric, and may not photograph as appetizing. Guardrail: use warm bone paperboard, a friendly flavor line, and a clear gummy macro image in digital product pages.

**Hero-film prompt:** “9:16 cinematic daily performance ritual, matte carbon 30-day supplement dispenser with signal green numbered sachets, a clean desk becomes a gym bag then a sunrise kitchen, one small translucent gummy daily pack, tactile paperboard and high-barrier sachet materials, high-end sports-tech editorial lighting, calm human hands, crisp modular composition, no logos, no readable text.”

## Option D — New-Age Botanical / Holistic Neo-Indian

### Strategic bet

**Working territory: SĀRA / Plants, precisely made.** This is an India-rooted, global-facing wellness brand that draws from familiar botanicals and food intelligence without using brown kraft paper, generic leaves, brass motifs, Sanskrit wallpaper, or “ancient secret” clichés. Its premium edge is radical provenance and contemporary sensory design.

**Who it recruits:** 23–45, conscious urban buyers in India and diaspora markets, interested in plant-based wellness but avoids overly traditional or pseudoscientific positioning.

**Brand voice:** Grounded, sensory, respectful, specific. “Known plants. Measured practice.” “Rooted in ingredients, not mythology.” “From kitchen wisdom to daily science.”

### Visual system

| Element | Blueprint |
| --- | --- |
| Master palette | Udaipur limestone `#E9E0CF`, monsoon ink `#173A38`, marigold pollen `#E9A52F`, jamun `#4A2B50`, tulsi `#667A54`. |
| SKU coding | Use a botanical pigment pairing: gut/tulsi, calm/jamun, energy/marigold, glow/pomegranate, daily/millet-sand. Keep illustrations abstracted and cropped. |
| Typography | Warm contemporary serif for the wordmark; accessible humanist sans for all information. Do not use decorative Devanagari or faux-classical fonts. |
| Mark | A simple seed-to-dot device / abstract “S” with no lotus, mortar-and-pestle, or ayurvedic seal. |
| Image world | Botanical photography as material studies: dew, seed texture, mineral pigment, fruit flesh, craft paper and clean glass—never a cluttered spice market tableau. |

### Pack architecture

**Primary form:** warm limestone opaque rPET jar or a stand-up high-barrier pouch for a family-format daily multi. Matte label, colored botanical-pigment band, and a small transparent/gummy viewing window only if it does not compromise UV/moisture protection.

**Front panel hierarchy:**

1. `SĀRA`
2. `CALM GUT` / `DAILY ROOTS` (plain-language outcome)
3. `AMLA + PREBIOTIC FIBER` / a precise named active line
4. `30 VEG GUMMIES`
5. Small `PLANT-BASED · QR PROVENANCE` line.

**Back panel:** “Plant profile” names the ingredient, part used, standardized active if relevant, origin, why it is included, and formula quantity. It should never imply traditional use alone equals clinical proof.

### Portfolio and commercial behavior

- First three SKUs: **Daily Multi (amla + core micronutrients), Gut (prebiotic + digestive botanical), Calm/Night (standardized botanical + appropriate conventional active)**. This avoids placing the whole value proposition on an unsubstantiated plant.
- Use regional flavor intelligence carefully: Alphonso mango, kokum, jamun, nimbu—only if the flavor fits the benefit and formulation.
- A small printed harvesting/provenance map or QR micro-story can elevate a D2C unboxing. Do not burden a Blinkit thumbnail with it.
- International version can lead with functional English naming and use India-origin provenance as a proof layer, not as an exoticized aesthetic.

### Failure mode and guardrail

Failure: collapse into generic Ayurveda, look masculine/herbal-pharmacy, or make vague “natural” claims. Guardrail: contemporary geometry, exact botanical/active quantities, food-safe color palette, and a formal evidence policy.

## 5. Decision matrix

| Criterion | A: Clinical Chic | B: Dopamine Wellness | C: Daily Ritual | D: Neo-Indian Botanical |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Immediate quick-commerce recognition | High | Very high | Medium | High |
| Premium price credibility | Very high | Medium | High | High |
| Differentiation in Indian gummy field | High | Medium | High | High |
| Social/creator velocity | Medium | Very high | High | High |
| Extensibility beyond gummies | Very high | Medium | Very high | High |
| Operational simplicity at launch | High | High | Medium-low | High |
| Risk of generic category resemblance | Low | High | Medium | Medium |

**Recommendation.** Launch an **Option A masterbrand with one Option B acquisition behavior**: clean clinical-chic pack architecture, but bright appetite cues in flavor imagery, creator content and trial sleeve. Develop **Option C** as the subscription/replenishment system after the hero SKU’s repeat rate is established. This avoids competing with Power Gummies/What’s Up using their most copied asset—bright jars—while retaining a path to short-form desirability.

## 6. Brand prototype development brief for the next stage

### The design team should create

1. One hero jar and three SKU colorways for Options A and B; one 30-day dispenser for Option C; one jar/pouch pair for Option D.
2. Thumbnail boards at 120 px, 160 px and 320 px placed beside 12 competing marketplace packs; record recognition time and benefit recall.
3. Physical mockups using actual jar silhouette, cap, label stock and seal—not only rendered cylinders.
4. A front/back content system with dummy regulatory copy, exact serving count, hypothetical dosage block and QR location.
5. Five social frames per route: shelf/thumbnail, hand-held, countertop, gym bag/commute, and a macro gummy shot.

### Go / no-go tests

- **Five-second test:** Can a consumer state the product type, primary benefit, format and brand without prompting?
- **Trust test:** Does the same consumer believe the pack contains a real functional dose rather than candy? Ask why.
- **Price test:** Does it credibly support the desired month-supply price without a secondary carton?
- **Heat/fulfillment test:** Does the actual format maintain gummy integrity across accelerated stability, distribution simulation and opening/reclosing use?
- **Portfolio test:** Can a new SKU be added by changing only one color band, product name and proof line while the brand stays unmistakable?

### Runway production note

Use Runway to establish lighting, material, social-world energy, and camera language—not to generate final labels. Generate pack shells with blank labels or abstract text blocks, select frames, then composite approved 3D/Illustrator packaging in post. This keeps typography, dosage, FSSAI information, and claims accurate.
