# Orelius research refresh

**Research date:** 12 September 2026
**Purpose:** Refresh the evidence used in the Orelius investor deck. This document separates verified product facts, benchmarks and assumptions, proposed product decisions, and open diligence.

## Executive finding

The opportunity is real enough to fund a controlled India-first validation phase. The evidence does not support an assumption that a Grüns-style gummy alone will scale into a billion-dollar Indian company. Indian incumbents prove that gummies can sell, yet they mainly use the format for targeted concerns such as multivitamins, hair, beauty, or energy. The proposed Orelius gap is a credible daily-nutrition ritual in a lower-sugar pectin format, supported by transparent testing and a retention system.

The gating variable is repeat purchase. In the existing cohort model, a ₹5 crore self-funded scenario produces approximately ₹20.8 crore annualised run-rate in month 36 under an optimistic US subscription-survival curve, but approximately ₹1.45 crore under a more conservative Indian repeat-purchase calibration. This deck therefore presents capital as staged behind retention evidence, not as an input that makes the outcome certain.

## Current public product evidence

### Grüns: the reference product

**Verified.** The current Grüns product page describes a 28-pack pouch with one 20g daily pack. The stated nutrition panel lists 50 calories, 15g carbohydrate, 6g fibre, and 8g added sugar per pack. It lists 20+ vitamins/minerals and an 8.7g Core Nutrients Blend containing tapioca fibre, inulin, mushroom, fruit, vegetable, botanical, and algae ingredients. The format uses pectin rather than gelatin. [Grüns product page](https://gruns.co/products/gruns), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** The public label makes Grüns useful as a format and category benchmark. It does not reveal its manufacturing process, the allocation of each component inside the proprietary blend, or a formula that Orelius may represent as its own. The 8-gummy, 8g-added-sugar serving does not fit the Indian product target proposed here.

### Man Matters: targeted daily multivitamin

**Verified.** Man Matters lists a 60-count multivitamin gummy at ₹749 on its direct site, with a stated two-gummy daily use. The product uses pectin and fructooligosaccharides and centres the proposition on vitamins and minerals. The product page also states that it should sit alongside a balanced diet and routine. [Man Matters product page](https://manmatters.com/dp/multivitamin-gummies/13410495), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** The product validates vegetarian pectin gummies and an accessible direct-to-consumer price point. It also illustrates the category norm: a concern-led, low-count vitamin format rather than a broad daily-nutrition system.

### Be Bodywise: women’s multivitamin

**Verified.** Be Bodywise lists a one-gummy daily multivitamin at ₹699. Its ingredient list includes FOS/chicory-root fibre, maltitol, pectin, vitamins and minerals, and it states no added refined sugar. Its own caution reads that the product is not for medicinal use and is not a substitute for a varied diet. [Be Bodywise product page](https://bebodywise.com/product/multivitamin-gummies), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** Low/no-added-refined-sugar positioning and a pectin base are established category expectations. Orelius must differentiate through daily-system value, transparent quantity testing, and a superior replenishment experience rather than simply being sugar-free.

### Siens by Dabur: corporate entry into D2C supplements

**Verified.** Dabur announced Siens in June 2025 as a premium D2C supplement brand. Its catalogue includes a Hair, Skin & Nails gummy. [Dabur launch release](https://www.dabur.com/press-releases/dabur-enters-nutraceutical-category-with-siens), accessed 12 Sep 2026. The live product page lists a 30-piece Hair, Skin & Nails gummy at ₹416 MRP and describes a beauty-specific proposition. [Siens product page](https://www.daburshop.com/products/siens-by-dabur-hair-skin-nails-3-in-1-gummies-30-pieces), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** Large FMCG entrants can reproduce a basic gummy format quickly. Orelius cannot claim a format moat; it needs an evidence, quality, and retention moat.

### Little Joys: clean-label trust system for children and mothers

**Verified.** Mosaic Wellness identifies Little Joys as its 2024 children’s-health brand. [Mosaic portfolio](https://mosaicwellness.in/), accessed 12 Sep 2026. Little Joys publicly describes age-specific gummies, zero-added-sugar positioning, batch-focused quality communication, and third-party heavy-metal testing. [Little Joys brand facts](https://ourlittlejoys.com/brand-facts/), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** This is direct India evidence that a transparent quality layer can form part of the product proposition. Orelius should adapt the principle for adults through lot-specific lab-report access, not make unverified certification or safety claims.

### Kapiva: premium Ayurvedic energy gummies

**Verified.** Kapiva currently lists a Shilajit Energy Gummy at ₹999 for 60 pieces, one gummy daily, and says the pack carries an NABL lab report. [Kapiva product page](https://kapiva.in/trending-products/kapiva-gummies/), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Investor implication.** Kapiva validates a premium price for a credible, sharply defined benefit. Its NABL-report practice offers an appropriate quality-benchmark reference, but Orelius must establish its own product-specific test programme.

## India feasibility synthesis

### What incumbent evidence supports

1. Consumers accept pectin/vegetarian gummies and prices from roughly ₹699 to ₹999 in current official examples.
2. Stronger brands add a trust or system layer: guided care at Mosaic brands, clear category identity at Kapiva, or visible quality communication at Little Joys and Kapiva.
3. Incumbents target a specific life stage or concern. This leaves a potential opening for an adult foundational-daily-nutrition proposition, but it is an inference rather than a proven unoccupied category.

### What incumbent evidence disproves

1. Gummies alone are not a defensible category moat.
2. A large ingredient list does not prove meaningful doses, absorption, or repeat purchase.
3. An attractive price does not establish lifetime value.

### Founder-level primary evidence

**Verified transcript evidence.** OZiva co-founder Aarti Gill said that, among Indian adult-nutrition customers, even scaled brands had seen at most two boxes a year. Source: `video-research/transcripts/oziva_aarti_gill_neonshow_full.txt`, lines 865–892, accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Verified transcript evidence.** Mosaic founder Revant Bhate described India’s vitamin/mineral supplementation market as below ₹10,000 crore. Source: `video-research/transcripts/decoding_d2c_revant_bhate_full.txt`, lines 911–920, accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Verified transcript evidence.** Plix co-founder Rishubh Satiya described CAC rising from a manageable cost to an unsustainable level as numerous competitors copied a proven category. Source: `video-research/transcripts/plix_satiya_zaveri_lifeboat_full.txt`, lines 1258–1269, accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Interpretation.** These are founder statements, not audited datasets. They are more useful than generic market-growth forecasts for establishing the downside case because the speakers have first-hand operating exposure. They should be represented as such in the deck.

## Product and formulation development brief

### Public benchmark: Grüns

Grüns combines a multivitamin/mineral system, prebiotic fibre, and a multi-ingredient fruit/vegetable/botanical blend in a pectin gummy. The current public panel provides ingredient categories and public label amounts only. It does not justify a direct formula copy or any claim that a gummy replaces vegetables, powder greens, or medical nutrition.

### Proposed Orelius target product profile

| Element | Proposed target | Rationale | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Format | 3–5 pectin gummies in a daily pack | Lower serving friction and lower sugar exposure than the Grüns eight-gummy serving; compatible with Indian category norms | Proposed target |
| Sweetening | No added sugar or demonstrably low added sugar; sensory validation required | Indian official competitor pages repeatedly use no-added-sugar positioning | Proposed target |
| Nutrition system | Transparent vitamins/minerals, fibre/prebiotic, and a limited, meaningful botanical/food blend | Avoid an opaque “kitchen-sink” blend whose doses cannot be evaluated | Proposed target |
| Label | Every active declared by amount; recommended use and warnings visible | Builds credibility and supports compliant review | Proposed target |
| Quality | Lot-specific assay, microbial, heavy-metal and stability evidence; QR or pack reference | Protects against the “candy with vitamins” critique | Proposed target |
| Packaging | 7-day trial and 28-day replenishment pack | Supports trial, reorder timing, and prepaid subscription testing | Proposed target |

### Formulation gates before commercialisation

1. **Ingredient eligibility:** a qualified FSSAI/regulatory professional must review every vitamin, mineral, botanical, sweetener, carrier, flavour, colour, and intended daily quantity.
2. **Dose design:** a qualified food technologist must set amounts against the applicable Indian rules and target population. Orelius must not use the public Grüns label as a dosage template.
3. **Stability:** test active retention at release and end of shelf-life under India-relevant heat/humidity conditions. “60°C heat-stable” remains a development objective until a finished-product study supports a specific claim.
4. **Assay and contamination:** validate label actives, microbiology, heavy metals, and relevant contaminants through a qualified laboratory. Lab results must represent the finished Orelius SKU, not a manufacturer’s generic capability.
5. **Sensory and adherence:** test flavour, texture, pack convenience, and willingness to reorder before increasing paid acquisition.
6. **Claims:** use structure/function and nutrient-content language only where permissible. Do not state prevention, cure, treatment, diabetes/weight-loss/GLP-1, or “greens replacement” claims.

## Regulatory and commercial boundaries

**Verified.** FSSAI’s current standards page identifies the 2016 Health Supplements and Nutraceuticals regulations as the current reference and also maintains the 2022 draft/operationalisation materials. [FSSAI health-supplements page](https://www.fssai.gov.in/standards/health-supplements), accessed 12 Sep 2026. The 2022 operationalised framework excludes products claiming cure, prevention, or mitigation of a specific disease unless specifically permitted and mandates key label disclosures. [FSSAI 2022 framework](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2022/03/6243ef28079ceDirection_Nutra_30_03_2022.pdf), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Verified.** ASCI’s current guidance requires advertising disclosures for material influencer relationships. Its 2025 health-and-finance guidance distinguishes generic promotion from technical advice; technical health information needs relevant qualifications and an upfront disclosure. [ASCI code and guidelines](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code-guidelines/), accessed 12 Sep 2026.

**Open diligence.** GST treatment remains unresolved for the precise Orelius product classification. The existing strategy documents a 5% versus 18% tension depending on classification/HSN treatment. A GST professional must advise before price, margin, or investor tax claims are final.

**Open diligence.** Sanode’s public site confirms WHO-GMP. The project does not independently confirm ISO 22000, HACCP, Halal, or US FDA registration. Do not state them as current Orelius or manufacturing advantages without certificates, issuer, scope, and expiry.

**Open diligence.** No trademark clearance for “Orellius” has been performed. Obtain a human-supervised India trademark search before public launch or investor circulation beyond confidential diligence.

## Economics and capital gates

**Benchmark / assumption.** The existing cohort model uses ₹699 AOV, ₹370–420 contribution margin before marketing, ₹350–600 CAC, and a prepaid D2C model. These are planning inputs, not Orelius performance.

| Scenario | Committed capital | Month-36 annualised run-rate | Use in deck |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Lean | ₹50 lakh | Fails in the model as fixed costs outlast cash | Lower bound; not a recommended funding plan |
| Moderate self-funded | ₹2 crore | About ₹5.5 crore | Evidence that a small durable business is possible |
| Sanode-backed self-funded | ₹5 crore | About ₹20.8 crore under optimistic retention | Upside case only |
| India-repeat stress test | ₹5 crore | About ₹1.45 crore under the conservative retention calibration | Required downside case |

The only scale gates that matter in the initial phase are: compliant final formula, finished-product quality data, prepaid mix, first-order contribution, Month-2/3 retention at or above 35%, and a repeat-purchase curve that clearly exceeds the two-boxes-per-year downside. A larger capital raise should follow evidence, not precede it.

## Deck conclusion

Orelius has a credible investor narrative when it presents a testable product and operating system: a lower-sugar pectin daily ritual, transparent finished-product testing, disciplined D2C acquisition, and a hard retention gate. The credible ask is capital for proof, not capital for an unsupported claim that a formula clone will outrun India’s existing consumer-health constraints.
