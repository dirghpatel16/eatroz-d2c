## Sleep-specific (highest priority)

### Wakefit
- **Color:** "Dawn and Dusk" palette — Ocean Boat Blue (#0075C2) + Pumpkin/orange (#F87614). Explicitly framed around the emotional register of pre-sunrise/pre-sunset light, not a clinical-sleep palette.
- **Logo:** "The Infinity Home" mark (2023 rebrand) — an infinity-loop device symbolizing a home/brand that "evolves with the needs of people," applied across retail stores, packaging, and web.
- **Type:** Not surfaced in sources; no distinctive claim found.
- **Pattern:** Rebrand was executed as one coordinated move across online + offline + packaging + retail simultaneously, not a logo-only refresh — worth noting as an execution pattern, not just an asset.
- **Memorable thing:** Positions itself as a life-stage/home-comfort brand ("simplifying living for a better quality of life") more than a sleep-science brand — mattress-company breadth, not supplement-grade specificity.
- Source: [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/wakefitco-unveils-new-brand-identity), [Brand the Change](https://brandthechange.com/rebrand/wakefit-co-unveils-fresh-brand-identity-introduces-new-brand-logo/), [SchemeColor](https://www.schemecolor.com/wakefit-logo-colors.php)

### Beam (US sleep supplement — cross-market comparison)
- **Positioning:** Founders explicitly aimed to be "the Nike of Wellness" — sport/performance-brand ambition applied to a CBD/sleep-adjacent supplement, not a spa/wellness register.
- **Packaging philosophy:** "Colorful, digestible package design that simplifies and de-complicates information but is full of energy and movement that's uncommon in the [supplement] space" — explicitly a reaction against the sterile-clinical default of the category.
- **Flagship product device:** Dream (sleep powder) is framed as a hot-chocolate ritual (cocoa + coconut milk powder, mixed with hot water/milk) rather than a clinical capsule — the format itself is the brand device, turning "take your magnesium" into an evening ritual.
- **Memorable thing:** Makes a supplement into a bedtime ritual object (a hot drink you make, not a pill you swallow) — the format *is* the brand differentiation, not the packaging graphics.
- Source: [Packaging World](https://www.packworld.com/home/article/21204041/new-cbd-packaging-for-beam-simplifies-product-story), [shopbeam.com](https://shopbeam.com/products/dream)

### Casper (mattress — brand identity case study)
- **Agency:** Built with Red Antler — explicitly "modern and a touch cheeky," designed for consistency across every touchpoint.
- **Logo:** Deep-blue wordmark with a signature "swoop" on the C, which doubles as a standalone favicon/social-avatar mark on its own — a wordmark engineered to also function as an icon.
- **Type:** Rounded, bubbly, near-3D letterforms in print — deliberately soft/lightweight rather than sharp or clinical.
- **Packaging:** Crisp white boxes with bold blue stripes, wraparound horizontal logo lockup — shipping-box-as-billboard, built to look good in an unboxing photo/video.
- **Advertising device:** Illustrated, cheeky subway/billboard campaigns carry the brand's personality more than the product photography does.
- **Memorable thing:** A single consistent "friendly, clever, blue" system deployed with total discipline across every surface (logo, box, ads) — the discipline is the differentiator, not any one asset.
- Source: [Medium/Lingo](https://medium.com/@lingoapp/case-study-how-casper-took-the-mattress-world-by-storm-ab528a15951b), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/print/casper-s-illustrated-subway-ads)

### Sleepy Owl (Indian D2C, not sleep-supplement — craft benchmark)
- **Color:** Bold blue + red + white on cold-brew packaging — described directly as color *ownership*: "blue now belongs to Sleepy Owl in the way that red belongs to Coca-Cola" within Indian coffee. A rare, named example of a single Indian D2C brand claiming a hue category-wide.
- **Logo:** A stylized owl with a heart-shaped negative-space counterform worked into the silhouette — functions across blue/green/red/neutral variants without losing recognition (a flexible-but-consistent mark system, compared favorably to Nike-level sophistication in one audit).
- **Type:** Rounded, friendly letterforms; palette otherwise deep browns (coffee) + cool blues (calm) + neutral beiges (balance).
- **Pattern worth stealing:** Brewing instructions and usage education are integrated directly into the pack design, not left to a separate insert — the package functions as a how-to, not just packaging.
- **Memorable thing:** Deliberately avoids "flowery artisanal" language in favor of "colour, humour and ease" — approachable/millennial rather than reverent-about-the-product, executed with genuine design discipline (a flexible identity system, not just a cute mascot).
- Source: [Marketing Monk](https://www.marketingmonk.so/p/how-sleepy-owl-revolutionized-the-coffee-experience-in-india), [Confetti Design audit](https://confetti.design/audit/sleepy-owl-brand), [The Hard Copy](https://thehardcopy.co/brand-case-study-how-sleepy-owl-built-its-brand/)

## Broader Indian D2C / wellness

### HealthKart / HK Vitals
- **Original system:** A signature teal box around the wordmark tied every SKU to parent brand HealthKart — but as the range grew, teal stopped distinguishing *sub-brands or products* from each other.
- **Redesign (with Moshi Moshi):** Bright, fresh colors + playful geometric patterns tailored per product, unified by one consistent grid system across the range. Deliberate move "away from the clinical and into something more optimistic" to de-intimidate nutraceuticals.
- **Memorable thing:** A range-identity system — one grid, many product-specific colors — solving the "how do 40 SKUs stay one brand but still look distinct" problem, which is directly relevant if Eatroz ever expands beyond one SKU.
- Source: [Packaging of the World](https://packagingoftheworld.com/2024/04/hk-vitals-x-moshi-moshi-media-llp.html), [Thought Over Design](https://thoughtoverdesign.com/work/hk-vitals)

### Kapiva
- **Logo:** A blocky, enlarged "K" monogram in black-and-white containing the wordmark — a deliberate departure from Ayurveda-category clichés (no mortar-and-pestle, no leaf-script imagery).
- **Packaging devices:** Two recurring graphic devices — an illustrated hand (reinforcing "Sourced without Shortcuts") and an arrow linking product name to benefit. Watercolor illustrations replace product photography on-pack.
- **Color:** Solid color-blocking per product/category, tying ingredient to function at a glance.
- **Memorable thing:** "Modern Ayurveda" made literal in the mark itself (a stripped-down monogram, not a heritage motif) — proof that an Ayurvedic-adjacent Indian brand can look contemporary without borrowing sleep/wellness's usual soft-pastel register.
- Source: [Opposite portfolio](https://oppositehq.com/portfolio/kapiva-branding-packaging/), [The Hard Copy](https://thehardcopy.co/kapivas-recipe-for-modern-ayurveda/)

### Wellbeing Nutrition
- **Logo format:** Rendered as a stamp — deliberately signaling "verified/certified" as a piece of visual shorthand, aimed squarely at ingredient-skeptical nutraceutical buyers.
- **Pattern:** Recognized as one of the most design-sophisticated Indian nutraceutical brands specifically for *packaging-format innovation* (e.g., melts/strips as a delivery format, not just a bottle) plus a multi-audience sub-brand architecture (different visual treatments for different product lines under one master system).
- **Memorable thing:** Trust communicated through the *shape of the mark itself* (a stamp) rather than through copy — a direct, portable idea for a category (gummies) that also fights an efficacy-skepticism problem.
- Source: [Confetti Design audit](https://confetti.design/audit/wellbeing-nutrition), [Thought Over Design](https://thoughtoverdesign.com/work/wellbeing-nutrition)

## Synthesis: what's crowded vs. open in the sleep category specifically

### Crowded
- **Mattress/sleep-hardware brands lean either tech-credential-led (Wakefit's home-comfort breadth, The Sleep Company's SmartGRID science story) or friendly/cheeky-consistent (Casper's blue wordmark system).** Neither register is "calm/ritual/bedtime" in tone — both are closer to lifestyle or engineering brands than to a bedtime-ritual mood.
- **Red/black "intensity" branding (MuscleBlaze, Boldfit's newer mark) dominates Indian sports/fitness-adjacent supplement branding** — the opposite end of the spectrum from sleep, but worth naming as the loud pole Eatroz should stay far from, same conclusion as the first research pass.
- **"Verification/trust" signaled through a graphic device (Wellbeing Nutrition's stamp) and range-wide grid systems (HK Vitals) are becoming a real pattern in Indian nutraceuticals** — Eatroz's tree-ring/mineral-cross-section motif already has a comparable "proof device" logic; this reconfirms (a third time, across two research passes) that a functional graphic device beats decoration.

### Open — genuinely uncrowded in sleep-supplement specifically
- **Nobody researched turns the supplement into an evening *ritual object* except Beam** — and Beam does it through product *format* (a hot-cocoa drink mix), not packaging graphics. No Indian sleep or supplement brand researched (across both passes) has built a ritual-format or ritual-timing device into the pack itself (e.g., visual cues for "take at X time," a wind-down sequence on the label, night-mode packaging that reads differently by lamplight vs. daylight).
- **No brand in this batch uses a genuinely "quiet at night" visual language** — dark-ground/low-contrast packaging designed to look calm under bedroom lighting (as opposed to Cymbiotika's dark-premium-for-retail-shelf register, previously logged). This is a distinct, still-open idea: design for the *actual physical context of use* (dim room, bedside table, last thing you see before sleep) rather than for shelf standout.
- **No sleep-specific Indian brand has built a Sleepy-Owl-style single-hue ownership claim.** Sleepy Owl owns blue in Indian coffee through sheer consistency; no researched sleep-supplement brand (Indian or US) has done the equivalent color-claim move for sleep. This is a concretely available differentiation, not a stretch — Eatroz could plausibly own a specific muted hue (e.g., a dusk-lavender or deep indigo, distinct from Wakefit's ocean-blue/pumpkin and from every "clinical blue" used elsewhere in the category) if it commits with Sleepy-Owl-level discipline.

**Single most useful cross-cutting finding:** the sleep-hardware category (Wakefit, Casper, The Sleep Company) is entirely occupied by either engineering-credibility or lifestyle-friendliness — none of it is *calm*. Combined with Beam's ritual-format insight, the open lane for Eatroz is a genuinely calm, low-stimulation, "designed to be the last thing you look at before sleep" visual language, executed with a single-hue-ownership discipline nobody in Indian sleep/supplements has claimed yet.
