# Orelius investor-deck design

## Purpose

Create a self-contained, evidence-first investor presentation for **Orellius**, a proposed India-first, superfood-gummy brand. It must show a credible route from product concept to a capital-worthy operating business. It is not a brand-name swap of the existing Roz deck and it must not present a literal Grüns copy as the strategy.

The deck will use Grüns as an externally observable reference point: its public product label, daily-format insight, and commercial model. It will identify which parts are unsuitable for India, formulate a differentiated target product profile, and state the conditions that must be proven before growth capital is justified.

## Audience and decision

Primary audience: early-stage and strategic consumer-health investors in India.

Decision sought: validate a staged investment thesis for Orelius. The deck should make clear that self-funded or tightly controlled validation capital is appropriate before a larger raise; no scale claim is presented as assured before repeat-purchase evidence exists.

## Core narrative

1. India has an everyday nutrition-adherence problem: pills and powders are inconvenient, while sugar-heavy candy-like gummies lack credibility.
2. Grüns validates a more attractive format, not a recipe that can simply be transplanted.
3. India’s leading brands prove demand in adjacent territories, while their models expose a gap for an evidence-backed, convenient daily ritual.
4. Orelius is designed to occupy that gap with a pectin-based, low/zero-added-sugar, heat-stable daily gummy and a transparent quality system.
5. The initial business is feasible only if product quality, compliant positioning, prepaid conversion, CAC, and Month-2/3 retention clear explicit thresholds.
6. Capital follows proof: initial validation creates the evidence needed for expansion capital, rather than relying on unsupported India-only billion-dollar exit claims.

## Presentation architecture

The output is a 20-slide deck plus 5–7 appendix slides. Slides should use a clean, premium analytical design with one warm brand accent, source footers on every factual slide, and minimal decorative imagery. Every quantitative claim must trace to `strategy.md`, the primary-source transcripts, a current primary source, or a clearly marked model assumption.

## Appendix

1. Public Grüns label comparison and explicit limitations.
2. Indian competitor teardown, including Man Matters, Be Bodywise, Siens, Little Joys, Kapiva, and relevant adjacent brands.
3. Regulatory and label-readiness checklist.
4. Cohort-model assumptions, retention stress test, and scenario outputs.
5. Detailed source list by slide.
6. Open diligence items: trademark clearance, GST classification, manufacturing certificates, final FSSAI formulation review.

## Formulation and claims guardrails

- The deck may compare **publicly disclosed** Grüns ingredients and label doses, but must not claim access to Grüns’ undisclosed manufacturing process, proprietary blend allocation, trade secrets, or clinical evidence.
- Orelius’ formulation is a target product profile for a qualified food technologist and regulatory reviewer to finalize. The deck must never imply that the target is already FSSAI-approved, clinically proven, or equivalent to a serving of vegetables/greens.
- No prevention, treatment, cure, weight-loss, diabetes, or disease-adjacent claims. Product claims require an FSSAI/ASCI review and ePAAS process where applicable.
- “60°C heat-stable,” certification, lab, and shelf-life claims must be supported by product-specific test evidence or presented as a development objective. Current public evidence confirms only Sanode WHO-GMP; other named certifications remain unverified.
- GST treatment is unresolved and appears as a diligence risk, not an assumed input.

## Research refresh protocol

The existing research provides the baseline. Before final production, refresh material claims that may have changed since 12 September 2026 using primary sources where possible:

- official product pages and labels for Grüns and Indian competitors;
- company press releases, filings, or acquisition announcements for ownership and revenue;
- FSSAI and ASCI primary materials for compliance;
- current marketplace list prices and pack sizes;
- saved founder transcripts for retention, CAC, and operating lessons.

Where sources conflict or cannot be independently confirmed, the deck will show the limitation plainly instead of selecting the more favorable number.

## Self-review

No placeholders are allowed except a deliberately unspecified future raise amount, which will be phrased as “capital to be determined after validation milestones” rather than left as a bracketed token. The product, regulatory, and financial boundaries are mutually consistent: the deck is a proposal requiring validation, not an assertion that a final product, approval, or scale outcome already exists. The scope is confined to an India-first investor deck and its directly needed appendix/source materials.
